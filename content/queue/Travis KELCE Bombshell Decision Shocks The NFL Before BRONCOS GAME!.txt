Fines are not decided by a referee. They are handed out during the week by the league office, and that number, five thousand two hundred seventy-seven dollars, is not somebody's guess. Now let me explain what the hip drop rule actually is, because it matters for the argument.

A hip drop tackle, in the league's own definition, is when a defender grabs a runner with both hands or wraps him up, and then unweights himself by swivelling and dropping his hips and lower body, landing on and trapping the runner's legs at or below the knee.

The league banned it because the injury data was alarming. It produced a rate of lower body injuries several times higher than a standard tackle, and the specific injuries it caused were the ones that end seasons. Ankles. Knees. Exactly what Travis Kelce was limping on.
