Let me unpack that, because that is a quarterback teaching you something and most channels will play the clip and move on.

There are two completely different ways to stop a run. Seattle does it with violence on the edge. The outside defenders attack, they refuse to let the play get outside, and they squeeze everything back inside into a pile. That is why Kenneth Walker got thirty-six yards. There was nowhere to bounce.

Denver does it differently, and it is arguably harder to beat. Everybody else on their front flows and fights and creates a mess, and Alex Singleton, the man in the middle, does not move. He waits. He lets the blockers commit, he lets the hole reveal itself, and then he arrives. Mahomes used the word funnel, and that is exactly right. The rest of that defense funnels the ball carrier to the one guy who has not committed to anything yet.

And that is why Singleton leads the entire National Football League with eighty-seven tackles, sixteen more than the next closest player in football. Patience is not a passive trait at linebacker. It is the whole job.

And here is how you beat it, since Mahomes raised it as the key matchup. You cannot out-leverage a patient linebacker with a wider run, because waiting is his whole method. What you do instead is make him wrong about which thing is happening. That is what play action is for. A patient linebacker who freezes to read the ball carrier is, by definition, a linebacker who is looking at the mesh point rather than dropping into his zone. Hold him there for half a second with a run fake and the intermediate window behind him opens. Which is precisely the window Mahomes named in the next sentence of his answer. He did not say those two things by accident.

Then Mahomes said the thing that is actually the plan for the rest of this season. Quote. "Whenever teams are going to try to stop Kenneth, we have to be able to counteract that. Kenneth is a special player and he makes a lot of big plays happen, and we're not going to stop trying to run the football, but if they're going to put so many resources into stopping him and playing a shell over the top, we have to be able to hit them in that intermediate area. And then guys have to be able to run after the catch and break tackles, and that'll put defenses in a bind where they have to decide what they want to take away."

Let me translate every piece of that.

A shell over the top means two safeties deep. Two-high. When a defense commits extra bodies to stopping the run and also keeps two safeties back to kill the deep ball, there is only one place left on the field. The intermediate area. Ten to eighteen yards, between the linebackers and the safeties. That is the window a two-high shell leaves open, every single time, because you cannot cover everywhere with eleven men.

And then the second half of what he said is the part that makes it work. Run after the catch and break tackles. Because a shell defense is designed to let you complete that intermediate throw and then tackle you immediately. Twelve yards and down. That is an acceptable outcome for them. It only becomes a problem if the man who catches it turns twelve into twenty-five.

Which, if you have been paying attention, is exactly the gap in this roster. And it is exactly why the front office is on the phone. Forced missed tackles after the catch is precisely what Kansas City's receiver room does not currently produce.

And then Mahomes finished with a sentence I want to read twice. Quote. "That's how you evolve throughout a season, as you continue to get better and better and learn from some games that aren't pretty, and hopefully don't have those scenarios again."
