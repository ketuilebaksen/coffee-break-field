Let me explain why a four-day week is a genuinely different physical event, because broadcasts treat it as a scheduling quirk and it is not.

A normal NFL week has the players doing almost nothing on Monday and Tuesday. That is not laziness, that is medicine. The deep muscle soreness from a football game peaks about forty-eight hours after it, and the soft tissue damage takes longer than that to repair. A standard week gives a body roughly six days to get back to baseline, and most teams still have men who are not right by Sunday.

On a four-day week, you get none of that. Monday is recovery, Tuesday is a walkthrough, Wednesday is a light practice, and Thursday you play. There is no real padded practice, which means no real installation of a new plan, which means you play Buffalo with whatever you already knew. And the soft tissue injury rate on short weeks is measurably worse, which is exactly the wrong risk for a team already managing a hamstring at receiver and a shoulder at cornerback.
