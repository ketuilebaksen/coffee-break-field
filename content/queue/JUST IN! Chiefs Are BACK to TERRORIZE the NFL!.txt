Let me explain what rushing yards over expectation actually measures, because it is the single most useful running back statistic that exists and almost nobody explains it properly.

Every carry in professional football has an expected outcome. The models look at where the defenders are, how many are in the box, the down and distance, the blocking scheme, where the holes are at the moment of the handoff. And from all of that they produce a number. On this specific carry, with this specific set of circumstances, an average NFL running back gains this many yards.

Rushing yards over expectation is simply the difference between what actually happened and that number.

So when a back ranks first in the league in it, the statistic is telling you something very precise. It is not telling you he has a good offensive line, because the line is already baked into the expectation. It is telling you that when you account for the blocking, the box count and the situation, Kenneth Walker is producing more yards than any other human being in football would have produced on those exact same carries.

And I want to be precise about why I care so much about this, because somebody in the comments is going to tell me I am complaining about a sixteen point win.

Moving the football between the twenties and finishing inside the twenty are genuinely different skills. Out in the open field you have the whole width and depth of a football field to work with. Inside the twenty, the end line becomes an extra defender. There is no deep part of the field left. Every throwing window shrinks. The safety help that was fifteen yards away is now five yards away. Play action matters less because there is less space behind the defense to threaten.

Which is why red zone offense is a specific competency rather than a by-product of being good. Teams that move the ball brilliantly and stall inside the twenty exist every single season, and in January they lose by three.

Kansas City has had the opposite reputation for most of the Mahomes era. This was the team that found a way, the team that scored when it had to. Right now, five games in, they are moving the ball better than almost anybody and converting it worse than they should.

The good news is that this is fixable in a way that a talent problem is not. Better short yardage execution with a healthier offensive line. A kicker finding his rhythm. One fewer forced throw into the end zone. None of that requires a trade.
