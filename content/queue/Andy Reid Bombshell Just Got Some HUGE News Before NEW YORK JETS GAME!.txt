Now let me explain why staying in bounds is not a detail, because this is a rule that decides plays and almost nobody knows it.

If an eligible receiver steps out of bounds, he becomes ineligible to be the first player to touch a forward pass. He can come back in, but until that ball is touched by somebody else, he is not legally allowed to catch it. And if he does, it is a penalty.

So when a cornerback walls a receiver out toward the sideline on a scramble play, he has not just covered him. He has removed him from the play entirely, as a matter of law. The quarterback running around behind the line now has one fewer legal target on the field, and he may not know it in real time.

That is why Reid used the words fight like crazy twice in one answer. It is not effort for its own sake. It is a receiver fighting to remain legal.
