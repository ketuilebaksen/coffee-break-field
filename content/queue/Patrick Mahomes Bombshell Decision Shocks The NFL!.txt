A courtroom and the National Football League are answering two completely different questions. A civil jury asks whether one person owes another person money, and it decides that on a balance of probabilities. The league asks something much broader and much vaguer. It asks whether a player's conduct is detrimental to the integrity of, or public confidence in, professional football.

Those are not the same bar. A player can be found not liable on eight counts and still be disciplined by the league, because the league is not bound by what a jury concluded and never has been. It runs its own investigation, reaches its own conclusions, and sets its own penalty.
