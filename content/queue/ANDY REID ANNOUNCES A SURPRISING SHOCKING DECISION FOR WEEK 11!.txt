Following. Which in football means shadowing. Not playing a side of the field, but travelling with one specific receiver wherever he goes, all game.

So last Sunday, Kansas City put Nohl Williams, a second-year third-round pick, on Atlanta's best receiver and told him to go everywhere that man went.

And here is why that is significant.

In most schemes, a cornerback plays a side. Left corner covers the left, right corner the right, and whoever lines up there is who you get. Simple, clean, and nobody gets badly exposed.

Shadowing is the opposite. The coordinator has decided one of his players can beat one of theirs, alone, all afternoon, and the rest of the structure bends around that matchup.

So you only do it when you trust somebody completely.

And Spagnuolo explained the history. He said they have done a lot of that in the past with L'Jarius Sneed, and with Trent McDuffie in a particular game.

Sneed and McDuffie. That is the company Nohl Williams has just joined in the way this staff uses him.

For the other guy. Not for the man shadowing. For his teammate.

Because if one cornerback travels with a specific receiver, the other cornerback no longer knows who he is covering. He has to be ready for whoever is left, so his preparation becomes vaguer and his assignments unfamiliar.

Happy feet.

Now let me explain that, because it is the single thing separating rookie quarterbacks from professional ones.

Happy feet is what happens when a quarterback becomes aware of the pass rush rather than the coverage. His feet start shuffling before there is a reason, because his body is anticipating a hit his eyes have not seen.

And the moment that happens his accuracy collapses, because throwing accurately requires a still lower body at release. A quarterback with happy feet throws from a platform that is already moving.

So a coordinator studying a rookie is not really looking at arm talent. He is looking for evidence that pressure makes the man panic, because if it does you can ruin his afternoon with four rushers and no blitz at all.

And Spagnuolo watched the tape and said: I don't see that. I think this guy's got pretty good control.
