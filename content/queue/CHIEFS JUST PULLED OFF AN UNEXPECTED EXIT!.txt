Chiefs Kingdom, a man who has started 29 games in the National Football League is not on this roster tonight.

Not injured. Not traded. Not retired. Just quietly gone from the 53, days before the biggest game of the year, and almost nobody said a word about it.

What's up everybody, welcome back to Coffee Break on the Field! Coffee in hand, because this is the exit nobody in national media bothered to cover, and once you see the numbers, you're going to wonder how it happened.

His name is Kader Kohou.

Now, if you only follow Kansas City, that name might mean nothing to you. So let me show you what walked off this roster. Three active NFL seasons. Twenty-nine career starts. Twenty-eight pass breakups. Three interceptions. Multiple forced fumbles. That is not a camp body. That is a starting cornerback in this league.

Here's the full résumé. He came into the NFL undrafted with Miami in 2022, and as a rookie he played 15 games, started 13, and finished with 72 tackles, 10 pass deflections, an interception and a forced fumble. Undrafted rookies do not do that. In 2023 he played all 17 games, started 16, and put up 63 tackles, 10 more pass deflections and a sack. In 2024 he added 45 tackles, eight more deflections, two picks and another forced fumble.

Before any of that, he was at East Texas A&M in Commerce from 2016 through 2021, where he was part of a Division Two national championship team during his redshirt season and piled up 111 tackles, five interceptions, four forced fumbles, three fumble recoveries and two defensive touchdowns.

So how does a player like that end up off a 53-man roster in September?

One word. Knee.

Kohou tore his ACL before the 2025 preseason and missed the entire season. Every snap of it. Miami didn't re-sign him once the 2026 league year opened, and Kansas City scooped him up on March 12th. Then, when this roster got finalized, he landed on the practice squad on September 1st.

That's the exit. A 29-game starter, off the active roster, in the quietest way possible.

And here's why I think it's smarter than it looks.

On paper, that's a demotion. In reality, Steve Spagnuolo now has a veteran cornerback sitting one phone call away who has lived inside at nickel, who understands NFL route combinations, and who can help on special teams the day he's activated. And Kansas City gets to watch that knee every single day in practice without having to depend on it tonight.

Because that slot spot is not about speed. A slot defender has to process motion, option routes, bunch formations, run fits and sudden leverage changes, all in about a second and a half. Kohou has already lived in that world for three years. You don't teach that in October.

There's a bonus too. While he's on the practice squad, he's giving this offense veteran looks on the scout team while learning Spagnuolo's system himself. He's helping Mahomes and the receivers prepare while staying ready for a call-up.

The honest caveat is the knee itself. Missing a full season affects burst, confidence and change of direction, especially for a defensive back. There's no guarantee he returns to his old level right away, and that's almost certainly why he's where he is. But if a starting corner goes down in October, Kansas City isn't starting from zero. They've got 29 starts on speed dial.

That's how championship rosters survive a season. And that's a real exit with a real plan behind it.

Now let's get to tonight, because there's a much bigger problem standing between Patrick Mahomes and a good night.

Josh Simmons has a good chance of missing this game, according to Andy Reid. And that matters more than any other injury on this team, because of who's walking into Arrowhead.

Denver produced 68 sacks last season, the most in the NFL. Nik Bonitto accounted for 14 of them by himself, fifth most in this league. And Bonitto normally attacks the left side.

That's Mahomes' blind side. In his first game back on a rebuilt knee.

Now, the surprise isn't that Kansas City needs a replacement. The surprise is who Reid says is first in line: Kahlil Benson. An undrafted rookie. A 24-year-old who spent six years in college, started on Indiana's undefeated national championship team, and fought his way onto this roster.

Genuinely great story. Here's the uncomfortable part. Benson played inside or at right tackle in college. He worked at right tackle all through camp and the preseason. And according to the reporting, he has not taken a snap at left tackle since before he enrolled at Indiana in 2020.

So picture it. Mahomes, first live football in nine months. A rookie taking his first meaningful left tackle snaps in six years. And across from him, a man coming off 14 sacks.

Benson might play great, and Reid sees every practice rep while we see none. But Kansas City already has another option with real left tackle experience, and his name is Jaylon Moore.

Go back to last season. When Simmons left the team for personal reasons, Moore started four games on that blind side. Kansas City went 3 and 1 and averaged 27 and a half points a game. That's not survival, that's an offense working.

And remember why he's here at all. The Chiefs signed Moore in March of 2025 to a two-year, 30 million dollar deal with the intention of making him the left tackle, before Simmons unexpectedly fell to them at pick 32.

Go back one more year. San Francisco, 2024. Moore started five games in place of Trent Williams, allowed one sack and five pressures, and earned a 74.9 grade from Pro Football Focus. His Kansas City grade dropped to 63 last season, fine, but he still gave up only three sacks on 257 pass-blocking snaps. And in five NFL seasons, he has two total penalties, none in the last three years. He also held up reasonably against Aidan Hutchinson in his first start last year, in a game Kansas City won scoring 30.

So why is Moore staying on the right?

Continuity. The plan was always Moore at right, Simmons at left. Moving Moore creates two changes instead of one, and coaches hate breaking up a side that's already communicating.

But here's my honest read. Denver doesn't need five sacks to wreck this. One early hit speeds up Mahomes' internal clock. One pressure kills a third down. One strip sack flips the night. And in his first game back, every clean pocket is worth double.

I'd move Moore. Take the matchup you can live with at right tackle instead of gambling on the one you can't.

The timing triggered the speculation. Dallas extended blocking tight end Brevyn Spann-Ford for three years right before Week One, while Schoonmaker was a non-participant in practice for a personal reason. Kansas City shows up in that projection for obvious reasons. Travis Kelce is deep into the back half of his career, Noah Gray is the presumptive successor, this team has always loved multiple tight end sets, and there's precedent, because the Chiefs acquired Peyton Hendershot from Dallas back in 2024.

The projected price is a conditional seventh-round pick.

And my answer is still no. A seventh is cheap, sure. But you'd be buying projection, not production, and if Dallas might release him anyway, Kansas City can wait and pay nothing. Cheap doesn't automatically mean smart.
