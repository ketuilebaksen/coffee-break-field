And if you are not sure what zone coverage even is, let me give you the thirty seconds that makes everything above make sense, because it is the foundation.

There are two ways to cover receivers. In man coverage, every defender is assigned a human being and follows him wherever he goes. Simple, aggressive, and brutal if you lose your matchup, because there is nobody behind you.

In zone coverage, nobody is assigned a man. Each defender is assigned an area of grass. He drops to his spot and covers whoever enters it. Zone is safer, it keeps everything in front of you, it allows eleven defenders to see the quarterback rather than chasing receivers' backs, and it is much harder to beat deep.

The weakness of zone is arithmetic. A defender can only cover one person at a time, no matter how big his area is. So if two receivers arrive in the same zone at the same moment, that defender is guaranteed to be wrong about one of them. He is not beaten by speed or by a route. He is beaten by a maths problem that the offence set up before the snap.

Flooding a zone just means doing that deliberately, with three or four players instead of two, and making sure they arrive at different depths so one defender cannot split the difference.

That is the whole art of attacking a zone defence. You are not beating a man. You are creating a geometry problem. You put more bodies into a zone than the defence has defenders assigned to it, and then one human being has to choose which of two receivers to cover, and whichever one he chooses is the wrong one.
