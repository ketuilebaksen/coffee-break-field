Today: the truth about Josh Simmons' timeline, what Brett Veach just did to Trey Smith's contract, and the players Kansas City could go grab if the young guys don't deliver. Let's get into it.

Let's start with the situation nobody in Kansas City wants to talk about out loud. Josh Simmons.

The left tackle has now been sidelined for over a month with a back injury. And as Week One gets here, the question everybody's been avoiding is finally getting asked: is Simmons actually going to be ready to play?

A Sports Illustrated report flagged exactly this issue, pointing out that the sheer amount of practice time Simmons has missed is becoming a real cause for concern. He hasn't practiced since the final week of training camp in St. Joseph. Think about that. That's not a couple of days off. That's a month.

Now, in fairness, it's not unusual for an injured starter to be held out when a team needs him healthy for the games that count. Teams do that all the time. But there's a detail here that changes the math.

Back injuries are complicated. They're not like a hamstring where you can look at a calendar and circle a date. Even when the pain doesn't seem severe, a back issue can linger for weeks, and it can get worse with physical exertion. That's what makes this scary, especially given the investment Kansas City has made in Simmons as their long-term blind side protector.

But let's give you the positive scenario too, because it exists. That same report notes the coaching staff might simply be resting him as a precaution, and there's still a real possibility he's ready for Week One. That's genuinely on the table.

The problem is what happens if it isn't.

If Simmons can't start the season, the expectation is that Jaylon Moore shifts over to left tackle. And okay, that solves the immediate problem. You have an answer for one Sunday. But the real concern isn't one game. The concern is if Simmons has to sit for several weeks.

Because a back injury doesn't need to knock a guy out for a game or two to become a much bigger issue. The wear and tear that accumulates over an NFL season can make it deteriorate. And think about the physical reality of the position. Offensive tackles are massive human beings, around 280 pounds, falling, blocking and colliding on every single snap. There is no such thing as taking a play off at that position.

That's exactly why Andy Reid decided to play it safe. Reid revealed that Simmons was examined for a spinal issue, that there's an area of inflammation, and that the team now has to wait for the situation to calm down. Simmons is still working, still doing his exercises and his rehab, but there is no definitive confirmation on when he returns.

And here's the most important point in this whole video: Kansas City cannot just wait indefinitely.

If Simmons is ready, great. Nobody's happier than me. But if he isn't, Brett Veach has to be prepared with a solution. And that leads us straight into the next part of this story, because while Simmons is a concern, this front office is already scanning other rosters for reinforcements.

The initial 53 is set, but that does not mean Veach is finished. The exact opposite, actually. The season is starting and Kansas City can absolutely still improve this roster.

Remember, this team took a chance on five undrafted free agents to round out the group. That shows real belief in young talent, and I respect it. But there's a risk baked into that belief. If those players don't step up quickly, Veach is going to have to look at the market again.

And there are positions that could clearly use reinforcement. Wide receiver. Safety. Interior offensive line. Those are the three groups where more depth would help right now.

Which is exactly why the practice squads of other franchises matter, because there's real talent hidden on them. Three names stand out.

The first is Maximus Pulley, a safety with the Philadelphia Eagles. Pulley played virtually the entire preseason and had strong moments in coverage. In August he allowed just one reception. One. And he also showed a willingness to come downhill, make tackles and help against the run. For Kansas City, he could be a genuinely interesting option as a backup free safety, which is a spot where you do not want to be thin.

The second name is Jordan Hudson, a wide receiver with the Dallas Cowboys. Hudson caught the eye at exactly the right time. In the final preseason game he put up four receptions for 97 yards and a touchdown. And it wasn't just catching. He showed blocking ability in the run game and contributed on special teams, which as we've learned this offseason is basically the price of admission at the bottom of a Chiefs roster. And there's one more trait that should interest Kansas City: Hudson has a history of showing up in big moments.

And then we come to a name a lot of you already know. Cedric Tillman. The receiver is currently on the New Orleans Saints practice squad, and he remains genuinely interesting for this team.

Because Tillman has the one thing Kansas City doesn't have much of in that receiver room: size, and a different skill set from everybody else on the depth chart. Plus he already has real NFL experience. He's not a project. So if these young guys don't deliver immediately, I could absolutely see Veach going after somebody who has already proven he can produce in actual NFL games.

But here's the part that determines whether any of this is even possible. Because Kansas City just found a way to create cap space before Week One, and it involves one of the cornerstones of this offensive line.

After finalizing the 53-man roster and the practice squad, the Chiefs had very little salary cap space left. So they made a strategic move involving Trey Smith.

According to Over the Cap, 18.54 million dollars of Smith's 2026 base salary was converted into a signing bonus. And as a result, his cap hit for this season dropped by roughly 12.5 million dollars.

The outcome is significant. Kansas City now has about 13.85 million dollars in cap space heading into Week One. That's not a massive war chest, let's be clear. But it's the difference between being able to respond to a problem and just having to watch it happen. It gives Veach flexibility to react to injuries, chase reinforcements, or pounce on an opportunity.

Of course, there's a cost, because there always is with restructures. You're not making money disappear, you're moving it into the future. Smith's cap hit for 2027 becomes roughly 12.14 million, while the remaining two years of that contract account for about 34.17 million in future cap impact.

And here's another key detail: Smith's 23.25 million dollar base salary for 2027 is already fully guaranteed. So it would not surprise me at all if the Chiefs and Smith end up back at the negotiating table next spring, working out a new extension to keep this cornerstone in Kansas City long term.

And honestly, he's earned that conversation. At 27 years old, Smith was franchise tagged last year before signing a long-term deal that averages around 23.5 million per year. But since then, the guard market has moved. Tyler Smith raised the bar to around 24 million annually. Peter Skoronski got to roughly 25 million. Which means Trey Smith now ranks tied for third at his position, and he remains absolutely vital to this offensive line.

So this restructure isn't really about saving money. It's about Kansas City creating breathing room to survive a long, unpredictable season.

And that's the thread that ties this entire video together. If Josh Simmons stays sidelined, if another injury hits that line, or if Veach spots someone worth grabbing off a practice squad, that 13.85 million dollars is what makes the answer possible. Without it, you're stuck hoping. With it, you can act.

The season is barely getting started, but one thing is already obvious: Brett Veach still has work to do.
