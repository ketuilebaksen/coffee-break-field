And before we move on, let me explain what a kicking slump actually is, because five missed field goals and an extra point is a real number and it deserves a real explanation rather than a joke.

Kicking is a repeatable mechanical motion, and when it goes wrong it is almost always one of three things.

The first is the plant foot. Where the non-kicking foot lands relative to the ball determines the entire swing path. An inch too close and you cut across the ball and pull it. An inch too far and you lose power and leave it short. This is the most common fault in the sport.

The second is the operation in front of him. A kick is three people, not one. Long snapper, holder, kicker, and the whole sequence takes about one and a quarter seconds. If the snap is slightly high, or the laces end up facing the wrong way, the kicker is adjusting at the exact moment he should be swinging freely. Nobody ever blames the holder.

And the third is what happens in a man's head after the second miss, which is the one nobody can coach.

Now look at Butker's season in that frame. Against the Chargers he missed an extra point wide left and a fifty-two yarder wide right in the same game. Then two more on Sunday night, one of them short.

Missing both directions and short is not one mechanical flaw repeating. That pattern usually means timing and rhythm rather than a broken technique, and the fix for rhythm is repetition and confidence rather than a rebuild.

Which is almost certainly what Andy Reid meant when he said Butker is off a bit and they have to fix it. He was not being evasive. He was describing a slump rather than a cliff.
