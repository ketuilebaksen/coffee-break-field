And let me explain why a hamstring specifically needs this exact progression, because it is the injury fans understand worst.

A hamstring strain is not like a bone or a ligament. There is no scan that tells you it is finished healing. Muscle tissue repairs with scar tissue that is less elastic than what it replaced, and the only real test of whether it will hold is to make it contract at full speed, repeatedly, on consecutive days, while the player is tired. That is exactly what a progression from limited, to no designation, to full participation is measuring.

And this is why re-injury rates on hamstrings are so brutal. A player who comes back at eighty-five percent does not usually re-tear on the first sprint. He re-tears on the twentieth one, in the fourth quarter, when the muscle is fatigued and the mechanics get sloppy. Which is why a staff that keeps a man limited on a Wednesday in November is not being overcautious. It is being correct.
