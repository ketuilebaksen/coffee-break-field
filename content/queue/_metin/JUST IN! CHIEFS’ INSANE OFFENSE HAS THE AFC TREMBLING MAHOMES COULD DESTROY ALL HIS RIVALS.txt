Now let me tell you exactly what that means physically, because "footwork" sounds like coach-speak and it is not. It is the most mechanical thing in football.

A quarterback throws from a platform. His back foot is the anchor, his front foot is the direction, his hips rotate through that line, and the ball leaves at the end of a chain that starts in the ground. When a quarterback drifts backwards in the pocket instead of holding his depth, three things happen immediately and all of them are bad.

One, his launch point moves backwards, which makes every throw longer than it was designed to be. A fifteen yard dig route from a clean platform becomes an eighteen yard throw, and eighteen yard throws arrive late.

Two, his hips open early, because moving backwards forces his weight onto his back side, and open hips pull the ball off target, usually high.

And three, and this is the one that destroys an offense, he breaks the timing with his receivers. Every route in this scheme is built on the ball leaving at a specific moment. If the quarterback leaves his spot a half second early, the receiver's break and the throw stop agreeing with each other, and now even the open man is a contested catch.

Remember that number from earlier in the week. Two for thirteen on throws into tight windows. Some of those tight windows were created by the coverage. Some of them were created by the platform.
