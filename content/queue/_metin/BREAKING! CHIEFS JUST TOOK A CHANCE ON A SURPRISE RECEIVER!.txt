An injury settlement. Here is what that actually is, because I guarantee you have seen the phrase and skipped over it.

When a player gets hurt and a team does not want to carry him on injured reserve for the whole season, the team can negotiate a settlement with him. Both sides estimate how many weeks the injury will take to heal, the team pays him for those weeks, and then he is released. He is free. He can sign anywhere once he is healthy, instead of sitting on a roster he is not going to play for.

There is one restriction. The team that settled with him generally cannot bring him back for a set period after the settlement window closes, which exists so clubs cannot use settlements to park players.

Let me explain the angle route, because it is one of the most elegant plays in football and it is the specific answer to a linebacker in man coverage.

The back releases outside like he is running to the flat. The linebacker covering him turns his hips toward the sideline to run with him. And then the back plants and breaks back inside, across the linebacker's face, toward the middle of the field.

A linebacker who has committed his hips to the sideline physically cannot recover. He has to stop, reopen, and change direction while the back is already running the other way with a head of steam.
