Every defence has to divide eleven men between stopping the run and covering the pass. A safety who drops deep is a safety who is not in the box. A safety who comes down into the box is a safety who is not covering anything deep. You cannot have him in both places, and every defensive call in football is some version of that trade.

Single-high coverage means one safety deep and the other one down near the line of scrimmage. That gives you an extra body against the run, usually seven or eight men in the box, and the price is that there is only one man protecting the back of the field. It is the aggressive call.

Two-high means both safeties stay deep. Nothing gets over your head, no fifty-five yard bombs, no explosive pass plays. And the price is that you are now defending the run with six or seven men instead of eight.
