And I want to explain why contested catches specifically are the thing to praise in a young receiver, because it is not a throwaway compliment.

Most rookie receivers get open some of the time. What separates the ones who play from the ones who sit is what happens when they are not open, because in the National Football League they are usually not open.

A contested catch is a ball thrown into a window where a defender has leverage, position or hands on the receiver. It requires three things simultaneously. Locating the ball while somebody is draped on you. Attacking it at its highest point rather than waiting for it. And holding on when the hit lands a tenth of a second later.

And here is why it matters so much for a quarterback. A quarterback who only throws to open receivers is a quarterback with four options on a given play. A quarterback who trusts somebody in traffic has eleven. That trust is the single most valuable currency between a passer and a receiver, and it is why Travis Kelce has caught nine thousand footballs with a linebacker hanging off his shoulder.

So when Patrick Mahomes singles out a rookie's catch over the middle in tight coverage, he is not being polite. He is telling the rest of the league, and his own coaching staff, that he is willing to throw that young man a ball he has no business completing.

That is how a role grows. Not in a highlight. In a Tuesday practice, over and over, until the quarterback stops hesitating.

Now, I am going to be the person who adds the caveat, because that is what this channel is for and because a number that extreme usually has a story behind it.

Passing yards allowed is a partly circumstantial statistic. It is not purely a measure of how well a secondary covers. It is also a measure of how often opponents chose to throw.

Think about how that works. If your defence is excellent against the run and your offence keeps games close, opponents keep running and keep the clock moving. If your defence gives up early leads, opponents stop throwing and start protecting the lead on the ground.
