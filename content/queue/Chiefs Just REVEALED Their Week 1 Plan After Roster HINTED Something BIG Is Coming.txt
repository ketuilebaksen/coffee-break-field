But while that situation is moving in the right direction, the Josh Simmons situation is moving into a completely different category for me.

At first, him missing practice didn't feel like panic material. You could understand the Chiefs managing a young tackle after a heavy workload. But now we're talking about an absence that has stretched out, and Andy Reid gave us more specific language about what's happening.

Reid said Simmons had his back checked, that there's a spot in there that flared up, and now they basically have to wait for it to calm down.

Read that again, because that's not "he's day to day." That's "we're waiting on his body." He has still been around the team, working out and doing rehab, so it's not like he disappeared. But the fact that he still isn't practicing is exactly why I'm watching this so closely.

And guys, this is where those recent moves start getting really interesting.

Kansas City added Joshua Ezeudu, who gives them flexibility because he can play both guard and tackle. They also brought in Diego Pounds, who has real left tackle experience from college.

Now, if this were happening with zero injury questions, you could look at those two trades and say the Chiefs are just strengthening their offensive line depth before the season. And maybe part of it is exactly that. But when your projected left tackle is dealing with a back flare-up at the same time you're adding multiple linemen who can give you options at tackle, I think it's completely fair to connect those dots.

Let me be clear about what I'm not saying. That doesn't mean Josh Simmons has been ruled out. He hasn't. It doesn't mean Kansas City already knows he can't play against Denver. What it means is that this front office is making sure they're not caught without alternatives if that back doesn't calm down fast enough.

And Reid even talked about the other guys doing a nice job, which matters, because the Chiefs have several ways they could handle this.

Jaylon Moore is one possibility at left tackle. Joshua Ezeudu gives you a flexible piece who can slide anywhere. Diego Pounds adds another tackle option. And Kahlil Benson is very much part of this offensive line picture too.

So instead of having one obvious answer, they've built themselves multiple possibilities. And honestly, that's my biggest takeaway from this entire week. Kansas City doesn't need to know today exactly which combination they're using. They need enough flexibility to adjust the moment they find out what Simmons can actually give them.

And protecting that left side becomes even more important when you remember who's walking back out there at quarterback.

Everything we've heard about Patrick Mahomes has been encouraging. Xavier Worthy was emphatic about it. His message was basically that Mahomes is back, and he talked about the work and the determination he's watched throughout this whole process. That's exactly what you want to hear after a knee injury ended his season.

But here's the thing nobody should forget: Mahomes did not play a single snap in the preseason. Week One is his first real game action coming back from that injury. Which makes the offensive line situation something I'm paying even more attention to. You want Mahomes back. You want him comfortable. And you absolutely do not want the opener turning into a game where the entire offense has to compensate for instability at left tackle.

And this is where Kenneth Walker's return connects to the bigger Week One plan.

The Chiefs have already shown signs of what they want to be. More under center. More play action. An offense that makes defenses respect what's happening in the backfield instead of playing pass on every snap. Having Walker available makes all of that a lot easier.

But it goes beyond one running back. Emmett Johnson has put himself in a really interesting position. His stock has gone straight up, and with the way this room developed, he looks like the clear number two right now.

And what I liked most was what Andy Reid said about his pass protection. That part gets overlooked because everybody wants to talk about rushing yards and explosive plays. But if you want meaningful snaps in this offense, you have to be trusted when the quarterback drops back. If Emmett Johnson can handle those responsibilities, then Kansas City doesn't have to change what it wants to do every time Walker comes off the field. That gives Reid freedom with the rotation, and it makes Johnson far more valuable than a backup runner.

Then there's Brashard Smith, who brings something completely different. He made the transition from wide receiver to running back, so naturally there are parts of the position he's still developing, and one of those areas has been recognizing pressure and handling protection. But he's been improving there, and his background gives this offense another type of weapon. I can see him being useful on third down, in matchup situations, in creative packages, and in the return game. He doesn't have to do the same things Walker and Johnson do to have real value.

And then look at the tight end room. Kansas City kept four tight ends, including Jake Briningstool. You don't keep a fourth tight end just to have another name on a depth chart. The Chiefs already use three tight end personnel, and keeping four gives them the ability to keep leaning into those heavier looks.

Now put all of it together.

Your starting running back is trending toward available. You've got a young number two whose pass protection the head coach went out of his way to praise. You've got a third back with receiver skills. You've got four tight ends. And at the same time, you have uncertainty at left tackle with a quarterback returning from a major injury.

To me, that adds up to an offense built with ways to take pressure off the offensive line, instead of just asking five guys to hold up while Mahomes drops back over and over again. Run the football. Get under center. Use heavy personnel. Create play action off of it. Make Denver defend different pictures.

That doesn't mean Kansas City suddenly stops being aggressive or becomes a run-only team. It means the personnel they kept gives Andy Reid the flexibility to play that way when the situation calls for it, and especially if Simmons isn't ready.

And there's a similar adjustment happening on defense that I really like.

L'Jarius Sneed worked in full, and that's genuinely encouraging given how carefully his workload has been managed all summer. He even wanted those preseason finale reps, because he felt like he needed real game work before the regular season starts.

But what stood out to me is where those snaps came from. He played six snaps, and five of them came from the slot.

That's interesting, because we already know from Reid that they're comfortable with Sneed playing inside or outside. And when you put a player with his physical style in the slot, you unlock a lot of options. He can cover from there, sure. But he can also become part of your pressure package. He's already proven that from inside, with 18 pressures and three and a half sacks in his previous work from the slot.

So I'm not looking at this as Kansas City declaring Sneed the full-time nickel. Chris Roland-Wallace is still very much part of that conversation. I see it as another option they can pull out depending on the matchup.

And there's a beautiful domino effect if they do use Sneed inside: it lets Chamarri Conner spend more time at safety instead of constantly being needed in the slot. That gives this secondary another way to line up without a big personnel change. Sneed himself sounds completely comfortable with it too. He said he likes playing nickel, likes moving around, and is willing to do whatever they ask. For a defense that wants to be multiple, a veteran like that is gold.

They built options at running back. They gave themselves multiple looks in the secondary.

That's not panic. That's preparation.
