All right. Decision two. The money. And this is the one nobody is explaining properly, so let me do it.

Getting Josh Downs onto a practice field took some work with the books. His cap hit came in at around one point nine six million dollars. And Kansas City was already tight enough against the cap that somebody's contract had to change.

So the second decision was to restructure Nick Bolton's deal. The Chiefs converted six point seven six million dollars of his salary into a signing bonus. And that freed up about three point three million dollars right now.

Now let me explain the mechanism, because once you understand it you will understand about forty percent of all NFL news.

Base salary hits the cap entirely in the year it is paid. A signing bonus does not. A signing bonus gets spread evenly across the remaining years of the contract.

So if you take six point seven six million dollars of this year's salary and call it a signing bonus instead, and the contract has two years left on it, then roughly half of that money stays on this year's books and the other half moves to next year. Six point seven six million, divided by two, is about three point three eight million. Which is almost exactly the figure that got freed up.

The player does not lose a penny. Nick Bolton still gets his money, and in fact he gets a chunk of it sooner, as a bonus, which players generally prefer. The team gets room today. And the cost is that Kansas City carries some of that charge into the years ahead.

That is the whole trick. It is a credit card. It is not free, and a team that does it too often ends up with a future full of money owed to players who are no longer there. But used once, to fit a receiver you need, on a contract that still runs through twenty twenty-seven? That is exactly what the tool is for.

And why Bolton? Because he is under contract through twenty twenty-seven, which means there are years to spread it across. You cannot restructure a man in the final year of his deal, because there is nowhere for the money to go.
